• Every charge rests until knockout. You cannot pull one early without emptying the kettle, so a charge's rest is its time to knockout and all charges share the same end.
  • A charge's start temperature is the wort temperature when it goes in. The longest rest starts at flameout; a shorter rest starts when its charge is added.
  • Between additions the wort cools passively at the batch cooling constant.
  • A charge colder than that curve implies a chill, which is inferred and shown in the summary. A charge above the curve would need heating, which the calculator refuses.
  • Chills are instantaneous. Afterwards the wort keeps cooling passively; holding a temperature is not modelled.
  • IBU shares split the whirlpool pool; each charge's mass is solved from its own rest and its own form multiplier.
