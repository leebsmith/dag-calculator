How the attenuation target works

The published attenuation is the strain's apparent ceiling. The cap is the high end minus the batch derate. The solver clamps the malt's fermentability to that cap; it does not force attenuation up to it.

Mash temperature is guidance, not a hidden input. 148–150°F suggests 0–1 derate points; 151–153°F suggests 1–3; 154–156°F suggests 3–5; 157°F+ suggests 5 or more. Enter the derate you intend.

STA1 is the vendor's QC result. It does not change the primary attenuation solve; the published attenuation cap is used for every strain. A positive result is shown for packaging decisions.

Sugar extract is never capped, so realized attenuation can rise above the selected malt cap. The above-cap row names the cause when that happens.

POF is the phenolic off-flavour phenotype and is unrelated to attenuation. It is shown only as a strain fact.
