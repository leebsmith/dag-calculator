The batch attenuation is solved from the grist composition and the yeast cap.

Why derate?

Assuming a healthy pitch, adequate oxygenation, and tight temperature control, derate the lab's maximum published apparent attenuation by 2–5 points.

Labs measure attenuation on a standardized, highly fermentable wort (typically 1.040–1.050 OG, 100% pale base malt, mashed near 149°F/65°C). Homebrew recipes rarely match that, so expect the middle or lower half of the published range.

Mash temperature. 148–150°F (64–66°C): 0 points. 152–154°F (67–68°C): 2–3 points. 156–158°F (69–70°C): 4–5 points. Higher rests leave more unfermentable dextrins.

Specialty malts. At 10–20% crystal, caramel, or roasted malt, add 2–3 points.

High gravity. Above 1.070 OG, add 2–4 points for the extra osmotic and alcohol stress on the yeast.

Worked example. An 80% strain, 10% crystal, mashed at 153°F: 2–3 points for the mash plus 2–3 for the malt = 4–6 points, so expect 74–76%.
