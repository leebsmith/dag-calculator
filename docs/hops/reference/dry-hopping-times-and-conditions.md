Dry hopping timing is defined by the intersection of **yeast metabolic state**, **extract attenuation**, **temperature**, and **oxygen mitigation**. Rather than simple calendar days, additions are categorized by the specific biochemical and physical conditions inside the fermenter.

---

## 1. Fermenter Timing Phases (By Metabolic & Attenuation State)

| Phase | Gravity / Attenuation Condition | Yeast & Physical State | Biochemical & Sensory Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **At Pitch (Day 0)** | 100% unfermented wort (Original Gravity). | Lag phase; high dissolved oxygen (DO) present for yeast growth. | Maximum glycosidase and $\beta$-lyase enzymatic cleavage of bound thiol and terpene precursors. High loss of volatile hydrocarbons (myrcene) via impending $\text{CO}_2$ scrubbing; hop polyphenols bind early with wort proteins to form stable colloidal haze. |
| **High Krausen (Early Active)** | 20% to 50% apparent attenuation. | Peak cell suspension; vigorous $\text{CO}_2$ evolution and convective mixing. | Rapid **biotransformation** (geraniol $\rightarrow$ $\beta$-citronellol; linalool/nerol conversions). Zero oxygen ingress risk due to positive $\text{CO}_2$ purge. Significant stripping of light, non-polar aroma volatiles and adsorption of nonpolar hop oils onto yeast cell walls. |
| **Mid-to-Late Fermentation (Spunding Window)** | 70% to 85% apparent attenuation ($1.020\text{–}1.014\text{ SG}$ or $2\text{–}4\text{ }^\circ\text{P}$ above terminal). | Yeast beginning to flocculate; moderate $\text{CO}_2$ evolution. | **The modern hazy/NEIPA standard.** Enough active yeast remains to scavenge trace $\text{O}_2$ introduced during the addition and drive secondary biotransformation, while reduced $\text{CO}_2$ velocity preserves volatile terpene fractions. Ideal window to seal/spund the vessel. |
| **Terminal Gravity (Pre-Flocculation / Warm)** | 100% apparent attenuation; pre-diacetyl rest completion. | Yeast still in moderate suspension; $\text{CO}_2$ evolution ceased. | triggers **hop creep** (amyloglucosidase and $\alpha$/$\beta$-amylase enzymes in hops hydrolyze residual dextrins into fermentable maltose/glucose). Because yeast is still warm and suspended, refermentation and subsequent **diacetyl (VDK)** precursor production are rapidly cleaned up. |
| **Post-Diacetyl Rest (Soft-Crashed)** | Terminal gravity; VDK below sensory threshold. | Flocculated and dumped (or settled) yeast following a drop to $50\text{–}58\text{ }^\circ\text{F}$ ($10\text{–}14\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$). | **The modern West Coast IPA standard.** Removing the primary yeast cake before hopping prevents yeast cells from stripping hop oils and eliminates cell-wall-mediated biotransformation, yielding pure, punchy, varietal-specific hop aroma with minimal hop creep. |
| **Cold Crash / Conditioning Phase** | Terminal gravity; stable conditioning. | Fully flocculated; beer held at $32\text{–}40\text{ }^\circ\text{F}$ ($0\text{–}4\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$). | Slows extraction kinetics of bittering humulinones and harsh polyphenols while still extracting key monoterpene alcohols (linalool, geraniol) within 24–48 hours. Suppresses hop creep enzymatic activity until the beer warms up. |
| **Packaging / Serving Vessel (Keg Hop)** | Finished, carbonated beer. | Clarified/packaged beer at $34\text{–}38\text{ }^\circ\text{F}$ ($1\text{–}3\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$). | Continuous cold extraction in a closed, oxygen-free environment (typically using bagged pellets, cryo/lupulin powders, or flowable extracts). Can impart grassy/astringent notes over weeks if vegetative matter remains in contact too long. |

## 2. Critical Environmental & Operational Conditions

Beyond the chronological point in fermentation, four operational variables dictate how a dry hop addition behaves:

### Temperature Regimes
* **Warm ($65\text{–}72\text{ }^\circ\text{F}$ / $18\text{–}22\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$):** Maximizes diffusion rates (complete extraction in 24–48 hours), increases solubility of heavier sesquiterpenes, and accelerates both yeast biotransformation and hop-derived enzymatic dextrin cleavage (hop creep).
* **Cool / Soft Crash ($50\text{–}58\text{ }^\circ\text{F}$ / $10\text{–}14\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$):** The equilibrium sweet spot for clean styles. Drops yeast out of suspension to prevent oil stripping, slows polyphenol extraction (reducing astringency and hop burn), and keeps hop creep enzymes sluggish while maintaining fast essential oil solvation.
* **Cold ($32\text{–}40\text{ }^\circ\text{F}$ / $0\text{–}4\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$):** Selectively favors polar terpene alcohols over nonpolar hydrocarbons, minimizes grassy/vegetative polyphenol extraction, and causes hop solids to settle more compactly (especially when paired with finings).

### Yeast Presence (On-Yeast vs. Off-Yeast)
* **In Presence of Suspended Yeast:** Nonpolar hop compounds (myrcene, humulene, caryophyllene) hydrophobically bind to yeast cell walls and are dragged to the bottom of the fermenter during flocculation. Simultaneously, yeast enzymes reduce aldehydes and convert monoterpene alcohols.
* **After Yeast Dump / Flocculation:** Preserves up to 30–50% more total hop oil in the finished beer by eliminating yeast-surface adsorption, delivering a sharper, more resinous/citrus profile rather than a rounded, fruity biotransformed profile.

### Pressure & Agitation Conditions
* **Atmospheric / Blow-Off Open:** Standard during active fermentation; carries risk of atmospheric $\text{O}_2$ ingress if opened post-fermentation without an active $\text{CO}_2$ blanket or positive purge.
* **Under Spunded Pressure ($10\text{–}15\text{ PSI}$):** Suppresses ester formation if applied early, traps volatile aromatics inside the headspace, and prevents nucleation/geysering (foaming over) when adding high-surface-area T-90 or lupulin pellets to $\text{CO}_2$-saturated beer.
* **Static vs. Dynamic Agitation (Rousing):** Static additions rely on Brownian motion and natural convection (often leaving a floating raft or dense bottom cone). Active $\text{CO}_2$ rousing, pump recirculation, or closed-loop dosing reaches peak oil saturation in 4–12 hours instead of 48–72 hours, though over-shearing pellets with a pump can shred vegetative matter and increase colloidal astringency ("hop burn").

### Multi-Stage / Split Conditions (DDH)
* **Charge 1 (Biotransformation + $\text{O}_2$ Scrubbing):** Added at $75\text{–}85\%$ attenuation ($66\text{–}68\text{ }^\circ\text{F}$) using high-geraniol/bound-thiol cultivars (e.g., Cascade, Centennial, Mosaic, Citra) to leverage active yeast metabolism.
* **Charge 2 (Pure Aroma Saturation):** Added post-terminal after a soft crash ($55\text{ }^\circ\text{F}$) and yeast dump/settling using concentrated lupulin pellets (Cryo, Lupomax) or high-oil varietals to layer raw volatile aromatics without further yeast stripping.

When clock time (days or hours) is removed, dry hop contact duration is governed by **state-based termination criteria**—physical, biochemical, and operational milestones that dictate when the hops have achieved their target effect or must be separated from the beer.

---

## 1. Biochemical & Metabolic Termination Milestones

| Contact Duration Defined By | Start Condition | Termination Trigger (End of Contact) | Brewing Objective & Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **To Attenuation Target** | Pitch or Early Krausen | Beer reaches a specific gravity milestone (e.g., $80\%$ apparent attenuation or $1.016\text{ SG}$). | Captures early glycosidase/$\beta$-lyase enzymatic cleavage during peak yeast metabolism, then drops the trub/hop load before terminal flocculation so early vegetative matter doesn't sit through conditioning. |
| **To Terminal Gravity (Primary Completion)** | High Krausen or Mid-Fermentation | Apparent attenuation ceases across two consecutive gravity readings ($\Delta\text{SG} = 0$). | Keeps hops in suspension only as long as natural $\text{CO}_2$ convection keeps them agitated; once fermentation halts and hop solids settle into the cone, they are dumped with the primary yeast harvest. |
| **To Hop Creep (Refermentation) Exhaustion** | Mid-Fermentation or Terminal Gravity | Secondary attenuation caused by hop-derived $\alpha$/$\beta$-amylase and amyloglucosidase enzymes completely plateaus. | Prevents post-packaging overcarbonation or bottle/can bombs by maintaining warm contact until dextrin hydrolysis and subsequent yeast refermentation run their full course. |
| **To VDK (Diacetyl) Clearance** | Any active or post-terminal addition | Total vicinal diketones ($\text{diacetyl} + \text{2,3-pentanedione}$) drop below sensory threshold ($\text{less than } 20\text{–}30\text{ ppb}$) via forced diacetyl test. | Because hop creep restarts yeast valine/leucine synthesis (producing $\alpha$-acetolactate), contact is maintained at warm conditioning temperatures until the yeast fully reduces the resulting diacetyl into flavorless acetoin and 2,3-butanediol. |
| **To Biotransformation Equilibrium** | Active fermentation | Peak conversion ratio of geraniol/nerol into $\beta$-citronellol and release of bound polyfunctional thiols (3MH, 4MMP). | Terminates contact once yeast-mediated enzymatic biotransformation plateaus, preventing prolonged yeast-cell-wall adsorption from stripping the newly formed terpene alcohols. |

---

## 2. Mass-Transfer & Equilibrium Contact Regimes

Instead of waiting a fixed number of hours, contact ends when the physical solvent-solute system reaches a specific thermodynamic or extraction boundary:

* **To Solvation Saturation (Partition Equilibrium):** Contact until the dissolution rate of polar monoterpene alcohols (linalool, geraniol, $\alpha$-terpineol) into the hydro-alcoholic beer matrix equals the rate of adsorption onto suspended solids (trub, yeast, and spent hop cellulose). In dynamic/recirculated systems, this state is reached almost immediately upon complete mechanical dispersion; in static systems, it coincides with full pellet disintegration.
* **To the Polyphenol / Humulinone Extraction Threshold:** Contact terminated just before slow-diffusing, higher-molecular-weight compounds—specifically catechins, epicatechins, proanthocyanidins, and oxidized alpha acids (humulinones)—cross the sensory threshold for grassy astringency or colloidal "hop burn."
* **To Target $\Delta\text{pH}$ Ceiling:** Dry hopping raises beer $\text{pH}$ linearly with dosage rate (typically $+0.10\text{ to }+0.35\text{ pH}$ units) due to proton binding by hop solids and vegetative buffering. Contact is halted (via yeast/trub dump or transfer) once the beer hits its upper target $\text{pH}$ ceiling (e.g., $\text{pH } 4.50\text{–}4.60$) to avoid soapy bitterness and microbial instability.

---

## 3. Physical & Mechanical Phase Milestones

In unitank and closed-transfer workflows, contact duration is frequently dictated by physical phase changes and mechanical separation events:

* **Single-Pass / Inline Dispersion (Flash Contact):** Beer flows through a hop cannon, torpedo, or inline dosing vessel during circulation or transfer from fermenter to brite tank. Contact duration is defined strictly by **total volume turnover** (e.g., 1 to 3 tank-volume turnovers through the hop bed) rather than clock time.
* **To Pellet Disintegration & Sedimentation (Flocculation Drop):** Hops are added statically to the top of the vessel; contact is purely the **gravitational settling transit time** required for the pellets to swell, break apart, pass through the liquid column, and compact into the bottom cone/trub bed, at which point they are immediately purged or racked off.
* **Thermal Transition Span (During Cooling Ramp):** Hops are dosed at the start of a soft crash or full cold crash, and contact lasts exclusively for the **thermal ramp duration** (e.g., the time required for the jacket or chiller to drop the thermal mass from $68\text{ }^\circ\text{F}$ to $34\text{ }^\circ\text{F}$), followed immediately by fining/racking once target temperature is reached.
* **Charge-to-Charge Displacement (Inter-Stage Span):** In multi-stage dry hopping (DDH/TDH), the first charge's contact duration is defined by the arrival of the second charge—Charge 1 remains in contact only until the vessel is prepped (soft-crashed and cone-dumped) to receive Charge 2.
* **Full-Lifecycle / Depletion Contact (Keg or Cask Conditioning):** Hops (bagged pellets, whole cones, or flowable/lupulin dosing) remain in the serving vessel for the **entire consumption lifecycle** of the batch—terminating only when the keg blows or the cask is kicked.
